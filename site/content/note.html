title: This week's note eyebrow: Note heading: NVIDIA doubled its revenue. The indicators still failed on it. date: 28 August 2026

NVIDIA reported second-quarter results on 26 August: revenue of $96.2bn, up 106% on a year earlier, with data centre revenue of $89.0bn, up 117%. It guided the current quarter to $108bn. By any measure the business had an extraordinary year.

Here is what the four indicators scored on NVDA in this week's run:

RSI scored 1.0, which is the floor. That is the same rule the live paper portfolio trades: buy when the fourteen-day RSI drops below 30, sell when it recovers through 50. Applied to NVDA, out of sample and after costs, it produced a Sharpe ratio of −0.96 — it lost money, reliably, across a period in which the company more than doubled its revenue.

That is the whole argument of this project in one stock. The indicator has no opinion about NVIDIA. It cannot see revenue, margin, or guidance. It sees a number derived from recent prices, and a rule that buys dips in a stock that mostly went up will sell the winners early and buy the falls that keep falling. Being right about the company and being right about the rule are different things, and only one of them is being tested here.

This is not a view on NVIDIA. Nothing here says the shares are cheap, expensive, or worth owning. It says that four specific technical rules, tested the same way as every other stock in the universe, did not survive costs on this one. A Survival Score is a statement about a rule's past behaviour, never a forecast about a share price.
